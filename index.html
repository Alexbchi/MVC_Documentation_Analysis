Recommended Minimum Viable Company scope

The proposed MVC retains the minimum people, technology, data, suppliers, and controls needed to maintain revenue collection, customer commitments, regulatory obligations, and safe operations during a disruptive event.

1. Critical activities and justification

Order fulfilment is the highest-priority capability because it converts committed demand into customer delivery and relies on a concentrated set of operational dependencies. Disruption would quickly affect revenue recognition, service levels, and customer confidence.

Supporting evidence: Business Continuity Plan v3.2, section 4.1; Operations Handbook, “Dispatch control” procedure. Source-level citations remain linked to imported documents in the workspace.

Payment processing is essential for cash collection and is dependent on a single gateway provider. A loss of service would cause an immediate operational and financial backlog.

2. Impact assessment

Impact dimensionAssessmentMaterial concern
FinancialHighDelayed invoicing and payment settlement.
OperationalCriticalOrder intake, fulfilment, and support are interdependent.
Legal & regulatoryHighMandatory reporting and data-protection obligations continue.
Human & safetyMediumEssential site roles require safe coverage and access.
ReputationalHighService interruption affects contracted customer commitments.

3. Dependencies and single points of failure

Payment gateway, customer master data, and the release-approval team are identified as single points of failure. The MVC should prioritize verified alternatives, recovery testing, and delegated authority for these dependencies.

4. Recommended next steps

Validate the activity register with process owners, confirm recovery objectives and supplier commitments, test the highest-risk dependency scenarios, and obtain executive approval for the resulting MVC scope.
